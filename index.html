GORIOT

A Korean Adaptation of Balzac's Masterpiece

A young law student from the provinces discovers that Seoul's elite run on betrayal when he befriends a ruined father whose chaebol daughters have bled him dry—and a charismatic fixer who offers him a darker path to the top.

Three Versions

Same Source, Different Stories

Version A

Contemporary Seoul

2025 · Gangnam · Gosiwon

A law student from Busan rents a room in a crumbling gosiwon. His neighbor: a retired noodle distributor who sold everything to marry his daughters up. Now they're in Gangnam and he's in a coffin-sized room. When the student begins an affair with one daughter and a devil's bargain with a disbarred prosecutor, he learns how Seoul really works.

Parasite meets King Lear in the glass towers of Gangnam.
Version B

IMF Crisis Era

1997-98 · Economic Collapse · Jjokbang

December 1997. A textile subcontractor who supplied Hyundai for 30 years watches his business collapse overnight. He sells everything to keep his daughters in their Apgujeong apartments while he moves into a jjokbang. A young banker, freshly laid off, becomes his unlikely witness as Korea's middle class evaporates.

The betrayal that built modern Korea's class divide.
Version C

K-Drama Dynasty

2025 · 16 Episodes · Family Empire

Go Yeong-sik ran a regional ramyeon distributorship—comfortable, respectable, deeply middle-class. He liquidated it all to buy his daughters' way into marriages above their station. Now 70, he signs over his last asset to fund his granddaughter's wedding. Three generations: the father who sacrificed, the daughters who spent, the granddaughter who inherits the silence.

SKY Castle meets Succession. The family saga Korea deserves.

What It's Really About

Class Immobility

헬조선 · "Hell Joseon"

Korea's "dirt spoon vs. gold spoon" reality. The statistical impossibility of upward mobility through merit alone.

Filial Piety Inverted

Confucian values weaponized. The father sacrifices everything; the children consume without gratitude.

The Education Trap

Why Balzac, Why Korea

Why Balzac

Balzac wrote Père Goriot in 1835 about a specific problem: what happens when money replaces birth as the measure of status, and parents bankrupt themselves to push their children into a class that will never fully accept them.

Why Korea

Korea has a word for this: 금수저/흙수저 (gold spoon/dirt spoon). The postwar generation built businesses, saved obsessively, and spent everything on education and wedding dowries. Their children married into higher circles—and learned to be embarrassed by where they came from.

The boarding house where Goriot dies among strangers is the gosiwon. The daughters who won't return his calls are in Cheongdam-dong. The young man choosing between conscience and ambition is in every law school in Seoul.
